# Routes a person can read: a named order, one meaning per label, and {depot} in a default's group

Design yardr-asc9, proposed 2026-10-23. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A route says which group takes the beads of a stage; a group is who works it (a pool of agents, a script, people, or another yard). A route with no depot is a yard default and serves every depot that does not state its own route for the same beads; a route that names a depot is that depot's own. A route's match key is which beads it takes: its stage, type, labels, bead priority and originator. A depot's effective routes are its own routes plus the defaults none of them sets aside. An estimate label is the label a shaper puts on a bead to say how hard (`difficulty:1`, `difficulty:2`, `difficulty:3`) and how large (`size:small`, `size:medium`, `size:large`) it is. A harness is the agent program a group runs (`claude`, `muse`, `codex`, ...), named by the group's `kind`. These are the words of design yardr-w6mh (`docs/design/2026-10-16-yardr-w6mh-depot-routes.md`) and of `docs/glossary.md`; this design builds on w6mh, which has landed.

## 1. The question

After the w6mh migration (yardr-8c10, 16 Oct: 108 routes to 57) mckean asked whether routing is usable or should be revisited. The yardmaster's reading, agreed with mckean the same night: more usable, three things left. First, "priority numbers carry the logic. Routes use 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10, and the winner is the higher number; the migration had to move the claude and muse defaults from 3 to 4 only to avoid a tie with difficulty:3. Intent cannot be read from the numbers." Second, "one label, several meanings": `muse` goes to a different pool in different depots; "does a label name a pool, or a kind of worker that each depot maps to its pool?" Third, "per-depot copies of one shape": the `approved` routes to each depot's assembly group and the release stages to each depot's release group; "template in the group, or a depot attribute that defaults name." And: the rule the yardmaster keeps in memory (route by difficulty: Opus, then Muse, then Kimi) and the live table disagree; "the design should leave one place that says where work goes." Notes added since ask for the same thing from the other side. mckean, 20 Oct: "one place to say e.g. d3 = Opus else Muse". mckean, 23 Oct: "we have the router that takes care of that, maybe need to be more scientific about it". For that, `yardr route report` should show the time a bead waits for a group apart from the time it is worked, and the outcome per label in one place, so the mapping from label to group is read from numbers. The yardmaster's note of 19 Oct names the week-start gap: a `Weekly<target+10` condition sends nothing to Opus in the first hours after a weekly reset, and a condition cannot express a floor. Breaking changes are fine.

## 2. What exists today

**Ranking.** `SortRoutes` in `internal/store/routing.go` orders routes by `Priority` (higher first), then `RouteSpecificity` (how many filters a route sets: type, depot, bead priority, each label, a named originator), then an own route before a default, then route id. `MatchRoute` takes the first route in that order among the bead's depot's effective routes (`EffectiveRoutes`, implemented in `internal/shared/match.go`). `RankLeg` names the leg that placed one route below another, and `yardr route explain <bead>` prints it. `checkRouteTie` refuses a route that states the same key at the same priority as a route of the same scope. `route add --priority` and `route set --priority` write the number, and `route list` prints it as `PRI`.

**The live table, 23 Oct 2026** (`yardr route list --json`): 74 routes, 16 defaults and 58 own. Since the w6mh migration, a release line, a peer yard (`airy`) and quota retunes have added routes. 26 routes carry a non-zero priority, in eight values: 1 (2 routes), 2 (5), 3 (4), 4 (3), 5 (2), 6 (2), 10 (6), 11 (2). What each value does, read against the ranking:

- 11 and 10 in `approved`: footplate's and yardr's `approved, type pr` routes to `decisions` sit at 11 only because the plain `approved` route beside them sits at 10 (footplate's note: "outranks footplate-assembly (10), which must not run merge-local on a pr bead"). With both at 0 the typed route wins by specificity. Signalbox has the same pair without the 11: its `approved, type pr` route to `decisions` (`4bca4a7jrey221y5zxae`) sits at 0 under a plain route at 10, so it has never won. No signalbox pull-request bead has existed yet, so nothing has gone wrong, but the first one would be landed by the merge script. The same holds for signalbox's `new, type issue` route (`4ba9ys5m8wcprpbzhck6`), which loses to the depot's plain `new` route at 2.
- 10 on yardr's `open, type train` and on the `brakeman-review` default: no route of the same stage competes for those beads, except a pull-request bead that also carries `brakeman-review`.
- 6 on the `airy` routes ("6 outranks the harness-label routes at 4"), 5 on `draft, type design, label claude` and on `review-security, label difficulty:3`, 4 on yardr-hosted's own `muse` route, 3 on the design-draft default and on footplate's and yardr's own `muse` routes, and 1 on the `difficulty:1` and `difficulty:2` defaults: each is already the more specific route among those that can meet one bead, so the number repeats what specificity says.
- 4 on the `claude` and `muse` defaults, against 3 on the `difficulty:3` default: both ask for one label, so specificity cannot separate them. The number says that a label naming who works the bead outranks a label saying how hard it is. This is the one rule the numbers carry that the ranking has no word for.
- 2 on the plain `new` routes of signalbox, yardr.dev and aiquokka (to `muse-light`), between the `difficulty:1`/`difficulty:2` defaults at 1 and the `difficulty:3` default at 3: in those depots easy work goes to `muse-light` and hard work follows the yard. This is the one rule that depends on the number's exact value, and no order of filter kinds can express it.
- 2 on yardr-hosted's own `difficulty:1` and `difficulty:2` routes: already more specific than every competitor.

Few of the 26 carry a note that explains the number: the `airy` routes, footplate's `approved, type pr` route, and the w6mh migration's notes on the `claude` and `muse` defaults and yardr-hosted's `muse` route.

**Labels as kinds of worker.** Groups `muse-builders` (limit 5), `muse-light` (2), `muse-hosted` (3) and `shapers` run the same harness, model and arguments (`kind=muse model=muse-spark-1.3-contributor`, `yardr group list`). They are pools, not different workers. Each exists so that one set of depots cannot take all the slots of another. `muse-light` serves signalbox, yardr.dev, aiquokka and yardr-design; `muse-builders` serves yardr and footplate; `muse-hosted` serves yardr-hosted. The `muse` label already resolves to the depot's pool through w6mh's mechanism: a default `new, label muse` to `muse-light` (`0dp4m8z9h10fvkckbar7`), set aside by own routes of the same key in footplate and yardr (to `muse-builders`) and yardr-hosted (to `muse-hosted`). `claude` is one default to `yardr-builders`, which runs `claude-sonnet-5-5`: the label names the harness, and the pool decides the model. The bead's `kimi` example is gone: `kimi-builders` and `kimi-security` are paused at limit 0 and no route names `kimi`. Nothing checks that a label named after a harness sends beads to a group of that harness. `docs/groups.md` ("The labels are yours") says yardr gives labels no meaning, while `DifficultyLabel` in `internal/store/eta.go` (the ETA cells) and `foldLine` in `internal/cli/bead_fold.go` already treat `difficulty:` and `size:` as estimates.

**Per-depot copies.** The routes that differ only in a group named after their depot:

| Stage | Routes | Groups |
|---|---:|---|
| `approved` | 8 | `<depot>-assembly` for signalbox, yardr.dev, aiquokka, footplate, yardr-design, yardr-hosted; `yardr-refinery` for yardr's plain route and its `type issue` route |
| `build`, `verify`, `publish` | 6 | `yardr-release`, `footplate-release` |
| `merged` | 2 | `signalbox-deploy`, `yardr-dev-deploy` (the depot is yardr.dev) |

A pack import already names a depot's agent and script groups `<depot>-<group>` (`docs/packs.md`, "Groups"), so the convention the copies follow is the import's. The import's record of which yard group took each pack group (`PackImport.Groups` in `internal/store/packs.go`, printed by `yardr pack list`) exists only for depots set up by import, and it is stale where routes were changed by hand: signalbox's record maps `assembly` to `yardr-refinery`, while its route sends `approved` to `signalbox-assembly`. There is no `group rename`; a group is renamed by adding the new group with the old one's settings, pointing the routes at it with `route set`, and removing the old one.

**Where the rule lives.** The difficulty-to-model rule is not in any yard file: the roles (`packs/starter/roles/yardmaster.md`, "Estimating a bead") say to estimate what a bead is and let the routes decide the group. The rule quoted in the bead is in the yardmaster session's own memory. The live answer is the defaults in `new`: `difficulty:1` and `difficulty:2` to `yardr-builders@Weekly<target else muse-builders` (Sonnet under target, else Muse), `difficulty:3` to `opus-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders`, plain to `yardr-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders`. Further routes in `draft` and `review-security` say the same for designs and security reviews.

**The report.** `yardr route report` (`internal/cli/routereport.go`) prints per group, originator and estimate label: closed beads, sent back, review fixed, security fixed, failed sessions, and MEDIAN BUILD (claim to leaving the stage). It has no depot column, no time spent waiting for a claim, and no line saying where the yard sends each estimate today. Its `best difficulty:N` lines take any sample size and any group that claimed outside review: since 16 Oct, `best difficulty:1` is `at-airy` on 2 beads, `best difficulty:3` is `kimi-builders` on 1 bead, and over the whole log `best difficulty:3` is `shapers`. The yardmaster's 23 Oct reading ("muse-light is 6x faster than muse-builders at the same outcome") compares two pools of one worker, so the gap is the depots' (small repositories against yardr's own gate), not the worker's.

**Quota conditions.** `ParseQuota` and `parseMargin` read `Weekly<80` and `Weekly<target+10`; `judge` compares the window's used percent with the number, or with `TargetPercent + margin`, where the target is the share of the window already passed. Comparisons joined by commas must all hold, and a group may be named once per route.

## 3. The proposal

Three changes and one addition to the report, each carried by a mechanism that exists. The priority number goes, replaced by a named order of the filters a route already has. A label keeps the one meaning it already has under w6mh (a kind of worker, mapped to a pool by each depot's own route), and the yard checks it. A default may name its group as `{depot}-<name>`, resolved where the effective routes are computed. The route table then is the one place that says where work goes, and the report puts the numbers beside it.

### 3.1 A named order in place of priority

`SortRoutes` ranks by these legs, each comparing what the route asks of a bead, the first leg that differs deciding:

1. **Urgency**: a bead-priority filter (`--bead-priority 0`, the P0 reserve). A hotfix may also carry a label (`claude`, `airy`) and must still reach the reserve, so this leg comes first (open question 7, decided 24 Oct). Two routes with overlapping thresholds (`--bead-priority 0` and `--bead-priority 1` both match a P0): the narrower threshold wins, then route id.
2. **Asked for by name**: how many of the route's labels are not estimate labels (`claude`, `muse`, `airy`, `brakeman-review`). Someone put that label on the bead to say who works it, and only urgency outranks it.
3. **Estimate**: how many estimate labels the route asks for. A route for `difficulty:3` and `size:large` comes before a route for `difficulty:3` alone.
4. **The rest**: type, depot and a named originator, one each, as specificity counts them today.
5. An own route before a default.
6. Route id.

Legs 4 to 6 are today's specificity and tie-breaks, unchanged. Legs 1 to 3 split the old specificity count into the three kinds of filter whose order the numbers were standing in for. The reserve this order protects is on the yard since 24 Oct: two default routes with `--bead-priority 0` and no quota condition, `new` to `opus-builders` and `review-security` to `yardr-security-heavy` (`review`'s default already runs without a condition). P0 alone: on this yard P1 means important, not urgent, and six P1 trial beads sit in backlog. An estimate label is a label beginning `difficulty:` or `size:`, the two prefixes `DifficultyLabel` and `foldLine` already read. That makes these two prefixes the yard's in routing too, and `docs/groups.md` says so in place of "yardr gives them no meaning".

`Route.Priority` is removed: `route add --priority` and `route set --priority` are refused as unknown flags. A store migration drops the field, and `checkRouteTie` refuses any second route of one scope that states a standing route's key, at whatever priority it was written. Without a number there is no "standby route at a lower priority"; a route that is not meant to win is removed. `route list` loses `PRI`. `route explain` names the new legs:

```text
$ yardr route explain yardr-x1
bead yardr-x1: stage new, depot yardr, labels claude, difficulty:3, size:medium, priority P2, originator mckean (the operator)
matches in yardr's effective routes, in order:
  1. 0dmyzkegd42qe20cw625  default  new, label claude  -> yardr-builders  WINNER
  2. 5qkytjh1rn5s5wp4ya0g  default  new, label difficulty:3  -> opus-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders  (below 1: asked for by name)
  3. 0dkjgrxm4sva5hnxxthv  default  new  -> yardr-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders  (below 2: estimate)
```

**What it changes, measured.** A replay of the live table (the 74 routes of section 2, the match and ranking reimplemented as `internal/shared/match.go` and `SortRoutes` have them) over every depot × stage × type × label combination: 10,360 probe beads that some route takes. 161 of them change group. Over the 866 real beads created since 16 Oct, each probed in stage `new` with its own depot, type and labels, 28 change: 22 in yardr.dev, 4 in signalbox, 2 in aiquokka. All 28 are `difficulty:1` or `difficulty:2` beads that go to `muse-light` today and would go to the `difficulty:1/2` defaults. This is the one rule in section 2 that depends on the number's exact value. Six own routes state it without a number: `new, label difficulty:1` and `new, label difficulty:2` to `muse-light` in each of the three depots. Added before the code lands, they win nothing (their priority 0 is below the plain route's 2, and they name the same group), so they change no bead's group. Under the new order they take those beads, so again no bead's group changes. With them in the replay, 71 probes still differ, in three shapes and no real bead:

- 28 probes: signalbox `approved, type pr` beads go to `decisions` instead of the merge script. The dead route of section 2 comes back to life, as footplate's and yardr's notes say it must.
- 3 probes: signalbox `new, type issue` beads go to `yardr-builders` instead of `muse-light`. The other dead route comes back to life.
- 40 probes: a bead labelled both `claude` and `muse` in yardr or footplate goes to the depot's `muse` route instead of the `claude` default. A bead that asks for two harnesses asks for nothing coherent; the depot's own route wins by leg 4, and `route explain` names that leg.

The replay script and its output are on the bead (note of 23 Oct). It counts only `difficulty:` labels as estimates and counts a bead-priority filter among the rest (leg 4), so it does not exercise legs 2 and 3 as proposed: no live route asks for a `size:` label or a bead priority, so its counts hold for this table, but the cases those legs decide are proved by bead 2's test table, not by the replay.

### 3.2 A label names a kind of worker; each depot maps it to its pool

The answer to the second question is the reading w6mh already built: a label such as `muse` names a kind of worker, and a depot says which of its pools does that work by stating an own route with the label's key. The default is the pool most depots use. The three `muse` pools are one worker in three pools (section 2), so "muse goes to three groups" is the mapping working, not three meanings. What is missing is that nothing holds the label to that meaning. A `muse` route could send beads to a `claude` group, and the label would then mean two things.

No code holds it (open question 4, decided 24 Oct: no check). `docs/groups.md` says it in one sentence: a label named after a harness sends its beads to groups of that harness, and a route that sends `muse` to a claude group makes the label mean two things. Nothing in the live table does so, and a mistake nobody has made is not worth a guard; `route explain` shows where a label's beads go. A check at `route add`/`route set` can be added the first time it bites. The same for two harness labels on one bead (section 4): the ranking resolves it, and the docs say so.

The model stays the pool's: `claude` to `yardr-builders` (Sonnet) and `claude` to `opus-builders` are both the label's meaning, and the depot or the stage picks. Labels that name no harness (`airy`, `brakeman-review`) mean what their route says, as today. The live table keeps one meaning per label: `claude` names only `yardr-builders`, and `muse` only the three muse pools.

### 3.3 {depot} in a default's groups

A yard default may name a group as `{depot}-<name>` (the literal text `{depot}`, then the rest of a group name), first group or further group:

```text
$ yardr route add --stage approved --group '{depot}-assembly' --note 'each depot lands its own beads'
$ yardr route add --stage build --group '{depot}-release'
```

The template is resolved where the effective routes are computed (`EffectiveRoutes`): for depot `yardr` the route reads `yardr-assembly`. A default whose resolved group does not exist does not serve that depot, as if it were not there, so a depot without `{depot}-release` keeps no route for `build`. `route list --depot` and `route explain` say so: `default <id> (approved -> {depot}-assembly) does not serve reports: no group reports-assembly`. The dispatcher, the flow view, `stageGroup`, the ETA cells and the board read the resolved route, because they already read the effective set. Events (`claimed`, `quota_wait`) name the resolved group. An own route may not use `{depot}`, since it knows its depot. `AddRoute` refuses a template that resolves to a group of no depot of the yard, and checks each resolved group as it checks a literal one (a quota chain names only groups that start agents). Deleting `yardr-assembly` leaves the default in place and stops it serving yardr; `group rm` says so beside the routes it removes.

The template follows the import's naming (`<depot>-<group>`), so a depot set up from a pack has its groups under the names the defaults expect. Applied to the table:

| Stage | Before | After | What it takes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `approved` | 8 own | 1 default to `{depot}-assembly` | renaming `yardr-refinery` to `yardr-assembly`. yardr's `type issue` route names the plain route's group and goes. The `type pr` routes to `decisions` stay own and win by leg 4 |
| `build`, `verify`, `publish` | 6 own | 3 defaults to `{depot}-release` | nothing |
| `merged` | 2 own | 1 default to `{depot}-deploy` | renaming `yardr-dev-deploy` to `yardr.dev-deploy` |

16 own routes become 5 defaults. Together with the six estimate routes of section 3.1 the table goes from 74 routes to 69, with no priority numbers left.

### 3.4 One place that says where work goes, and the numbers beside it

The route table is that place: the defaults in `new` are the difficulty-to-model rule, and `yardr route list --depot <d>` and `route explain` already read it out. Three things make it the only place:

- **The yardmaster keeps no copy.** Its memory note on routing by difficulty is deleted, and `packs/starter/roles/yardmaster.md` ("Estimating a bead") says: where an estimate goes is `yardr route list`; a change of mind is a `route set` with a `--note` saying why, not a memory.
- **The report names the mapping.** Under the first table, `yardr route report` prints one `now` line per estimate label, from a probe bead with that label and no depot (the defaults alone), naming the depots whose own routes set that default aside (`SetAsideRoutes`). These are the routes the numbers above it are judging:

  ```text
  now difficulty:1: default kda29j3vt5pevynvs6db -> yardr-builders@Weekly<target else muse-builders (own routes in aiquokka, signalbox, yardr.dev, yardr-hosted)
  now difficulty:3: default 5qkytjh1rn5s5wp4ya0g -> opus-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders
  ```

- **The report separates waiting from working, and pools from depots.** The report gains a WAIT column: per bead and visit to the stage, the time the bead was ready and unclaimed, and the median of that over the beads. A bead is ready from entering the stage (`advanced` into it), from its last blocker closing, from `unheld`, and from the end of a session that left it in the stage (`released`, `session_failed`, or a session that ended without a move); it stops being ready at `claimed`, `held`, or a new blocker. A bead held by a person or by a dependency is waiting for them, not for the group, so that time is not counted. A bead claimed twice in one visit, because its first session failed, adds both waits: the second wait is what the failure cost in time. MEDIAN BUILD keeps meaning claim to leaving. `--by depot` adds a DEPOT column beside GROUP, so two pools of one worker are compared within one depot. The `best` lines need at least 10 beads per group, and a group qualifies for them by claiming in a stage worked by builders, which rules out reviewer groups as now and shaping groups too. A best line with no group over the floor says `too few beads to compare`.

### 3.5 The week-start gap: a floor on a target comparison

Decided 23 Oct as (b) of open question 5 and done: aiquokka reports a cushioned target (aiquokka-0f75, a gradient from +10 at a reset to -10 at its end), read by every route. No floor grammar in yardr; the text below stays as the alternative that was not taken.

A target comparison may carry a floor, the least threshold it uses: `Weekly<target+10 floor 25` holds while the window is under the larger of target + 10 and 25 percent. In the first hours after a reset, when the target is near 0, it still lets beads through up to 25%; later in the week it is `target+10` as today. The floor is a third optional part of `parseMargin`'s grammar, read only after `target`. `judge` takes the larger of the two thresholds, and its reason says which one applied: "claude's Weekly window is 27% used, the route asks under 25% (the floor; target 3% + 10)". The alternatives are in section 5.

## 4. Left out

- **Keeping priority beside the named order** (as a last leg, or an escape hatch for a route that must win). Section 2 shows that every number but one either repeats specificity or says "named worker over estimate", which leg 2 now says. The remaining one is stated by six own routes. A number kept for emergencies would bring back the reading problem the moment someone used it. A route that must win states a more specific key.
- **A depot attribute naming its groups by role** (`yardr depot set yardr --group assembly=yardr-refinery`, defaults naming `@assembly`). It covers what the template covers without renames, and could also map the muse pools. But it is a second record saying where work goes, beside the routes, against the question's "one place". The record yardr already has for it (`PackImport.Groups`) is stale on this yard, which shows how such a record drifts. It is open question 3.
- **Templates for the muse pools** (`{depot}-muse`): the pools are shared across depots on purpose (one limit for four small depots), so a pool per depot would multiply groups and limits. The override of section 3.2 keeps four routes and the shared limits.
- **Two worker labels on one bead.** Refusing a second harness label when a bead is labelled, or ranking harness labels among themselves, is a rule for beads, not routes. `route explain` names the leg that decides, and the replay found no real bead with two. Open question 8 (24 Oct): allowed, the ranking resolves it, and `docs/groups.md` says so in a sentence.
- **An estimate required to leave backlog.** The report shows that unestimated beads are the worst predictors of their own cost. Requiring an estimate is a rule of the flow (a stage that will not let a bead out without one), not of routing. Under the named order an unlabelled bead goes to the plain route as today. It is a separate bead if wanted.
- **Changing where any estimate goes.** The numbers suggest moving `difficulty:1` off Sonnet (`yardr-builders`: 79 beads since 16 Oct, median 26 min, 7 failed sessions; `muse-builders`: 100 beads, 18 min, 0 failed). That is the operator's routing choice, made with `route set` once the report separates depot from pool. This design changes how routes are read, not where they send beads.
- **A flow filter, route names, depot sets**: left out by w6mh, for reasons that still hold.

## 5. Open questions

1. **The small depots' easy work.** Signalbox, yardr.dev and aiquokka send `difficulty:1/2` to `muse-light` and `difficulty:3` to the yard, through a plain route at priority 2. (a) Six own estimate routes, added before the code lands (section 3.1): no bead moves, and the table states the rule in filters a reader can see, at the cost of six routes. (b) Let the three depots' easy work follow the yard: 28 beads a week move from `muse-light` to Sonnet-under-target, else `muse-builders`, and the plain routes at 2 lose their purpose. (c) Make `muse-light` the `difficulty:1` default for every depot: a yard-wide routing change that the report, read without a depot column, cannot yet support. I would take (a). Decided 23 Oct: (a).
2. **The two signalbox routes that have never won.** (a) Keep both and let them win under the new order: pull-request beads in `approved` go to `decisions`, as footplate's and yardr's do and as their notes require; issue beads in `new` go to `yardr-builders`. No signalbox bead of either type has existed. (b) Keep the pull-request route and remove the issue route, so signalbox issues follow its plain `new` route to `muse-light`. (c) Remove both and keep today's winners. I would take (a). The pull-request route is the fix footplate needed; the issue route is what the contributors pack wrote, and the operator can remove it later with one `route rm`. Decided 23 Oct: (b), the issue route was never in use.
3. **Per-depot groups: template or depot attribute?** (a) `{depot}-<name>` in a default's groups (section 3.3): one resolution in `EffectiveRoutes`, the import's naming convention, two renames on this yard. (b) A depot attribute naming its group for a role, which defaults name: no renames and more general, but a second record of where work goes, and a new field, command and syntax. (c) Neither: keep the 16 own routes. I would take (a). Decided 23 Oct: (a).
4. **The harness-label check.** (a) Refuse, as section 3.2 shows: the label has one meaning that cannot drift. (b) Warn on stderr and add: the warning is gone with the shell. (c) No check, and a sentence in `docs/groups.md` only. I would take (a). Nothing in the live table fails it. Decided 24 Oct (left to the yardmaster): (c), a guard against a mistake nobody has made; bead 4 drops out.
5. **The week-start gap.** (a) A floor in the condition (`Weekly<target+10 floor 25`, section 3.5): the operator sets it per route, at the cost of a third part in the comparison grammar. (b) The limits source reports a target that starts at a few percent, rather than at 0, at a reset. No yardr change, but the claude limits source is a separate repository, and every route reading that target shifts together. (c) Nothing: Opus waits for the target to catch up for the first hours of each week. I would take (a). Decided 23 Oct: (b), done as aiquokka-0f75; bead 7 drops out.
